# Agent-native science course

This course helps researchers use coding agents throughout scientific work. Its language separates durable research practices from the capabilities of any particular agent or product.

## Language

**Best and biggest science**:
Scientific work of greater ambition, scale, and quality than a researcher could accomplish without coding agents. Greater output counts only when it improves understanding or scientific capability.
_Avoid_: AI productivity, more experiments

**Cumulative science**:
Science in which each round of work improves the starting position for later researchers, agents, experiments, or projects by preserving trustworthy knowledge, evidence, artifacts, and capability.
_Avoid_: Accumulating outputs

**Scientific system**:
The connected practices, artifacts, tools, context, and controls that turn research effort into work that can be inspected, trusted, reused, and extended.
_Avoid_: Tool stack, agent workflow

**Agent-native researcher**:
A researcher who treats coding agents as general-purpose collaborators and actively looks for ways to expand the scale and ambition of their work. Programming fluency is not a prerequisite.
_Avoid_: AI programmer, agent expert

**Coding agent**:
An AI system that can inspect files, use software tools, produce artifacts, and carry out multi-step technical work under direction.
_Avoid_: chatbot, coding assistant

**Research artifact**:
A durable output that another person or agent can inspect, verify, reuse, or build on.
_Avoid_: answer, response

**Reliable handoff**:
The condition in which another person or agent can understand, inspect, rerun where relevant, and safely extend scientific work from its artifacts.
_Avoid_: final answer, completed chat

**Scientific ambition and agency**:
A researcher's willingness and ability to attempt work that previously seemed blocked by missing programming skill, time, or implementation capacity.
_Avoid_: AI productivity

**Cumulative science**:
Science that leaves behind both stronger research capabilities and a better-supported state of understanding. Its engineering artifacts and epistemic record let future people and agents understand, verify, continue, and extend the work.
_Avoid_: More output, compounding engineering

**Research activity map**:
A non-sequential map of the kinds of work involved in research. Researchers can move among activities in nested feedback cycles at different timescales.
_Avoid_: Linear pipeline

**Orient**:
The research activity of developing a useful picture of the problem, field, prior work, constraints, resources, and unknowns.

**Frame**:
The research activity of selecting and expressing the question, decision, hypothesis, or uncertainty that currently deserves attention.

**Design**:
The research activity of making an intended inquiry explicit through its method, comparisons, measurements, controls, criteria, and expected interpretation.

**Run**:
The research activity of using a built or provisional capability to generate observations or results.

**Interpret**:
The research activity of updating scientific understanding from evidence.

**Communicate**:
The research activity of making work legible through notes, figures, explanations, status updates, papers, or conversation.

**Research discipline**:
A practice that keeps research trustworthy and manageable across many activities, such as provenance, versioning, reproducibility, or review.
_Avoid_: Stage, phase

**Inspectability**:
The quality that lets a person judge an artifact or process through a purpose-built representation such as a report, comparison, visualization, diff, test result, or trace.
_Avoid_: Visibility

**Reproducibility**:
The degree to which another person or agent can regenerate or independently check a research result from preserved inputs, methods, code, data, and environment information.

**Agent technique**:
A reusable way of directing or coordinating coding agents, such as context preparation, subagent delegation, skill use, or Git-based recovery.
_Avoid_: Research activity, research discipline

**Prototype**:
A provisional artifact made to expose or reduce uncertainty. It may clarify the question, reveal assumptions, test an idea, or suggest what to do next; it need not become durable infrastructure.
_Avoid_: Rough draft, minimum viable product

**Build**:
A research activity that creates a durable artifact which future experiments, claims, people, or agents can depend on repeatedly.
_Avoid_: Prototype, productionization

**Learning record**:
A compact account of a question or event, the evidence produced, what changed in understanding, and the resulting decision.
_Avoid_: Full transcript

**Learn companion**:
A course companion that reviews a working session for reusable lessons about the research, the workflow, and human-agent interaction patterns, then proposes changes for future work.
_Avoid_: Prototype retrospective

**Course companion**:
An agent behavior supplied by the course to guide a next move, audit scientific discipline, or extract reusable lessons from completed work.
_Avoid_: Grader

**Tool-agnostic**:
Applicable across coding-agent products, models, and payment models rather than tied to one vendor's interface.
_Avoid_: vendor-neutral
